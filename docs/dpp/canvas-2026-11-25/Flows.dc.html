Police and DPP work hand in hand. Each paper has a sender, a receiver and a reason.

Who gives what, who gets what

#
FROM → TO
WHAT TRAVELS, AND WHY THE RECEIVER NEEDS IT
IN THE APP TODAY
1
Police → DPP
The police file: forwarding minute, charge sheet (Police Form 53), statements, accused's statement, exhibit list, medical and expert reports, investigation diary, scans. Needs added: when the suspect was arrested and where held, so the DPP can see the 48-hour clock without a phone call.
Exists Add custody time
2
DPP → Police
Receipt: file received, ODPP registration number, or refused and why. The station stops wondering whether it arrived.
Exists
3
DPP → Police
after perusal
One of three answers. Sanction: the signed, dated, stamped charge sheet. Return for further inquiries: written advice listing what to do, a due date, and a draft amended charge sheet if the charge changes. Close the file: reasons.
Exists Add due date
4
Police → DPP
after a return
Resubmission: the new papers, tied to the file that was returned, not a new file.
Exists
5
DPP → Court
To open or move a case: charge sheet filed (magistrate), or indictment and summary of the case (committal to the High Court), withdrawal when charges stop.
Papers exist, filing by hand
6
Court → DPP
Court case number (written back on the ODPP file), hearing notices, adjournments and who caused them, rulings, judgment, sentence, record of proceedings for an appeal, notices of appeal or revision.
Exists with the Courts app
7
DPP → Police
after every hearing
Hearing minute: next date, which witnesses to summon, which exhibits to bring. The standards require this on the police file after each adjournment.
New
8
Police → DPP
before the hearing
Witnesses served or not served, exhibits secured: the DPP must know before walking into court, because a prosecution-caused adjournment counts against the file.
New
9
Public → DPP → Police
A written complaint is stamped, studied, the police file is called for, and the complainant is answered within a week.
New
10
Court → Prisons
the DPP only learns the result
Remand and production orders go court to prison. The DPP sees "not produced" when the court records it, because that is an adjournment not caused by the prosecution.
Exists via Courts
Everything travels as a signed package that works over postbox, e-mail or a memory stick. Police and Prisons never talk to each other directly. The DPP's view of the case is never overwritten by what another office sends: arrivals wait for a person to accept them.

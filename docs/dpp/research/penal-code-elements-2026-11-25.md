# Penal Code elements behind the DPP legal guide (2026-11-25)

Why this exists: the legal guide shipped with empty "what must be proved" lists, so Decide's checklist and Assist had no law to work from. This note records where the new starter content comes from, what is law and what is only practice, and what is still unchecked.

## Source read

- Penal Code Act, Cap. 120, full text, from WIPO Lex: https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/text/499410 (read 2026-11-25). Section numbers below are this edition's, which is the numbering Ugandan charge sheets use.
- The ULII 2024 revised edition (https://ulii.org/akn/ug/act/ord/1950/12/eng@2024-12-23) renumbers the Act (a search summary showed the general theft punishment as s. 244 there). I could not open it from this machine (403). The guide follows Cap. 120. **Open question for the office: which numbering does it charge under today?** If it is the revised one, every section reference in the guide has to be remapped from the revised text.
- Not read: case law on what each element means in practice (for example what counts as "breaking", or what an "assault" is). Those lines in the guide say only what the Act says.

## What the Act says, offence by offence

| Offence | Section | Maximum | Class |
|---|---|---|---|
| Theft | 254 (definition), 261 | 10 years, unless the thing stolen has its own punishment (cattle s. 264, wills s. 262, postal matter s. 263) | felony |
| Doing grievous harm | 219 | 7 years | felony |
| Assault occasioning actual bodily harm | 236 | 5 years | misdemeanour |
| Housebreaking / burglary | 295(1), 295(2) | 7 years; at night (burglary) 10 | felony |
| Breaking into a building and committing a felony | 297 | 7 years | felony |
| Criminal trespass | 302 | 1 year | misdemeanour |
| Conspiracy to commit a felony | 390 | 7 years, or the felony's own lower maximum | felony |

Definitions used (s. 2): harm = any bodily hurt, disease or disorder, permanent or temporary; grievous harm = harm that amounts to a maim or dangerous harm, or seriously or permanently injures health or is likely to, or extends to permanent disfigurement, or to permanent or serious injury to an organ, membrane or sense; maim = destruction or permanent disabling of an organ, membrane or sense; dangerous harm = harm endangering life; night = half-past six in the evening to half-past six in the morning; dwelling house includes any building or structure, or part of one, kept by the owner or occupier as a residence.

## Corrections made to the earlier starter

- "Breaking into a building, 295 and 297" was two offences under one name. s. 295 is housebreaking of a human dwelling with intent to commit a felony; s. 297 is breaking into a shop, store, office, school, place of worship or building next to a dwelling and committing a felony in it (s. 298 is the same with intent only). The guide now has them as two entries.
- Charge wording now reads "sections 254 and 261", as on real charge sheets, not "254, 261".

## What is law and what is practice

- **Proving** lines: the Act's elements in plain words, each with its section. Law, with one exception. "The accused is the person who did it" is added to every offence because identity must always be proved; it is not an element of the section.
- **Assault** (s. 236): the Act in this edition gives no definition of "assault" in s. 2, and I did not find one in the text I searched. The line says only "the accused assaulted the complainant". The office should add its working definition.
- **Papers** and **gaps** lines: practice written from the elements. They are a draft for the office and have not been checked against how any ODPP office actually perused such files. They carry no authority.
- **Medical report** is named in plain words; no police form number is given because I have not confirmed one from the form itself.

- **Correction to the second pass.** I wrote that the WIPO text of rape was out of date. It is not: the revised edition's s. 110 is word for word the Cap. 120 s. 123 (carnal knowledge of "a woman or girl"). Only defilement changed. The Cap. 120 s. 129 in the WIPO text ("a girl under the age of eighteen years", death) is replaced by s. 116: "a sexual act with another person below the age of eighteen", life; aggravated defilement (death, High Court) when below fourteen, offender HIV positive, parent or person in authority, victim with a disability, or serial offender.
- **Numbering is now mixed, on purpose and flagged.** These three entries use the revised numbers (110, 111, 116) because that is the only text of the amended offence I have; the other 17 entries use Cap. 120 numbers. Each of the three says so in its "words" lines. Rape was 123 in Cap. 120. The revised text also renumbers every other offence in the guide (defilement alone moved 129 to 116); a concordance for the 17 was started and not finished (the browser extension disconnected). **Owner decision still open:** which numbering the office charges under. When it is answered, remap the guide in one pass.
- Not read: case law on consent, on proof of age, or on the sentencing position after the death penalty provisions (the revised text still prints death for rape, aggravated robbery, aggravated defilement and murder). The guide says so in each entry.
- "Carnal knowledge" (rape) is not defined in the text read. The "sexual act" definition in s. 116(7) is for defilement only.

**Other cautions.** Murder (189) is printed as a death sentence, and robbery 286(2) and defilement likewise; the guide says "as printed" and tells the office to confirm the sentencing position it applies today, since I did not read the case law. "Aggravated robbery" is not a section of this edition: it is 285 with 286(2). Common assault: no definition of assault in the places read, as before. Papers and gaps lines are practice drafts for a prosecutor to correct, as in the first pass.

## Numbering decision, 2026-11-26: Cap. 120

The owner chose Cap. 120 numbering for the whole guide. Rape, defilement and aggravated defilement were therefore changed from the revised-edition numbers (110, 111, 116) to Cap. 120: rape 123, punishment 124, attempt 125 (all three **verified** in the WIPO Cap. 120 text); defilement 129(1), aggravated defilement 129(3) and (4). **The defilement numbers are not verified in Cap. 120 form:** the WIPO text predates the 2007 amendment and still has the old s. 129, so the subsection numbers are those of the revised edition's s. 116, which restates the amended section. They are consistent with a constant offset of 13 between the editions around this point (abduction 126 to 113, defilement 129 to 116). The two sections after it (child-to-child sex, compensation) are cited by description only, not number. Each of the three entries says this. A prosecutor should check them against the amended Cap. 120 (Act 8 of 2007). The earlier "mixed numbering" caveat no longer applies. The revised edition (Chapter 128) renumbers every offence in the guide; if the office later moves to it, remap in one pass.

## Steps

1. **Court:** the case CR 340/2026 is registered (aggravated robbery, High Court level). It is entered in Courts directly (the filing came from the DPP in case 1's way, not repeated here).
2. **Lawyer:** opens the matter with the court number and files a bail application at a **magistrate's** level wording. The registrar finds it is in the wrong place or form and **returns** it with a reason: "Bail for this offence is applied for in the High Court. Re-file there." (verify: aggravated robbery bail is the High Court's.)
3. **Lawyer finds out:** the firm's matter shows the filing "returned" with the reason in the registrar's own words.
4. **Lawyer re-files** the application correctly. Registrar takes it in.
5. **Court sits** on the application. The judge **refuses bail**: "The accused is likely to interfere with witnesses." The sitting is held with that outcome, and an order of refusal is issued.
6. **Lawyer finds out:** the court's record of the sitting reaches the firm as a note with the outcome (built 2026-11-24), and the order arrives with its wording.
7. **Next move:** the firm records that bail was refused and diarises either a fresh application on changed circumstances or an appeal. (Verify the route and time limits; the app counts days from its rules, so the firm should see a deadline running once the refusal is recorded.)

## Must be true

- The firm never has to guess: a returned filing shows the reason; a refused bail shows the words of the order and the court's record of the sitting.
- "Received by the registry" is not read as "granted" (wording changed 2026-11-24).

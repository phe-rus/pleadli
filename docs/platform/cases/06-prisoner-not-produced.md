# Case 6: adjourned because the prisoner was not produced

Status: script written 2026-11-24. Procedure from general knowledge, NOT checked by a practitioner. Fictional names. Follows case 5 (same accused, Okumu David, CR 340/2026, bail refused).

## What this case proves

A failure at the prison reaching the court and the firm: the court's remand order is refused by the prison for a stated reason and the court sees why; once recorded, the prison reports that the prisoner was not produced on the hearing day; the court adjourns naming the prisons as the responsible party; the advocate learns it with the reason. Courts, Prisons and Lawyers. Police and DPP are not in this case.

## Steps

1. **Court:** issues a remand order for Okumu David to a named prison and sends it.
2. **Prison:** imports it. The prison has no record of him, so it **returns** the order with a reason. The court sees the order "returned" with the reason.
3. **Prison:** admits Okumu David on the strength of the warrant.
4. **Court:** sends the order again. The prison records it against him; the court sees "recorded".
5. **Court:** fixes the next sitting (a mention).
6. **Prison:** on the day, the prisoner is not produced. The prison reports "not produced" with a reason (for example, no transport).
7. **Court:** imports the report, holds the sitting as **adjourned**, reason "prisoner not produced", responsible "prisons", with a next date, and tells the firm.
8. **Lawyer:** the matter shows the court's record "Adjourned. Prisoner not produced... Prisons..." on the hearing row, and the next date.

## Must be true

- A refused order shows its reason to the registrar, not just a status.
- The adjournment records who was responsible (the prisons) in the court's and the firm's records.
- The report from the prison does not change the case by itself; the court decides.

# Pleadli Prisons: review, context and plan (2026-10-08)

The user's direction, in their words: the Prisons side "is just not there"; "what is prisons supposed to be handling and doing, that context is missing"; "what do the HQs do"; "what does using Prisons do for Uganda Prisons, how would it be impactful"; "how would Apple do this"; "the jurisdictions, how will it work in Uganda, in Congo, in Kenya"; "DPP, the lawyers and police really brought up the concept, followed the structures of how things work and are operated."

This document is that context, in plain words, and the plan that follows from it. **No code is written for it yet.** The decisions at the end (section 9) need an answer, or "accept the recommendations", before the work in section 10 starts.

It does not replace the research. The statutory detail lives in `research/findings.md` (Prisons Act 2006 read in full, UPS statistics, Visiting Justices, promotions) and `research/capabilities-head-office.md`. What was missing was the step from that research to a product a prison officer and a Commissioner would recognise as theirs. That step is this document.

Evidence tags as elsewhere: [P] primary text read, [S] secondary source, [I] inference, [U] not yet researched (a backlog item, never "out of the product").

## 1. What the Uganda Prisons Service is, in numbers

| Fact | Figure | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| People held | 79,186 (Nov 2025); about 79,600 (Dec 2025) | [S] World Prison Brief, from UPS |
| On remand (not convicted) | 45.8%, more than 36,000 people | [S] |
| Official capacity | 23,184 | [S] |
| Occupancy | 341.6%: more than three people for every place | [S] |
| Prison units | 266 custodial units (UPS, Jan 2024); 270 (WPB) | [P] UPS report |
| Regions | 19 | [P] UPS news, Apr 2026 |
| Staff in post | about 14,000 of an approved 49,470 (29%) | [P] UPS staff review 2024 |
| Court productions | 36,239 in one month: 1,726 people taken to court every day | [P] UPS Jan 2024 |
| Prisoners per custodial officer | 4 to 9 across units | [P] UPS Jan 2024 |
| UPS's own system | PMIS: still a pilot in two large prisons (2024); link to the Judiciary "formative" | [S] |

What these numbers mean for the product: Uganda's prisons are full of people the law presumes innocent, held three to a place, by an under-staffed service that moves 1,700 people to court every day on paper. Almost every problem the Service is criticised for (overholding, remand past the constitutional limit, missed court dates, lost warrants, unanswered complaints, monthly figures counted by hand) is a **records and deadlines problem**. That is exactly what software can fix.

## 2. How the Service is organised (who decides what)

```
Prisons Authority (Minister chairs)      policy; appoints Assistant Superintendent and above
Prisons Council (CGP chairs)             appoints and promotes up to Principal Officer I; discipline
Commissioner General of Prisons (CGP)    head of the Service; Deputy CGP beside
  Headquarters, Kampala                  6 directorates, 23 departments
  Regional Prisons Commander (19)        supervises every prison in the region
    District Prisons Commander           district counterpart
      Officer in Charge (OIC)            runs one prison unit: the people, the records, the staff
```

| Level | What it does, from the Act | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| **Commissioner General** | Orders transfers between prisons (s.73); grants and recalls parole (s.89); releases habitual criminals on licence (s.87); sends the Minister a report on every prisoner serving life or more than 7 years at each four-year mark (s.88); publishes the prohibited-articles list posted in every prison (s.101); issues standing orders and administrative instructions (s.7(2), s.77(2)); receives Visiting Justices' extracts and Ministers' and Judges' observations (ss.110-111) | [P] |
| **Headquarters directorates** | Prisoners' administration, training, finance, personnel, legal affairs, research and planning, welfare and rehabilitation (s.13(1)(c)); health services (s.26); farms (3,618 staff) and industries; statistics and ICT | [P] |
| **Regional Prisons Commander** | Supervises all prisons in the region; implements policy; chairs the Regional Prisons Committee (every OIC in the region sits on it) and the Regional Promotion Board; is the channel from the OIC to the CGP | [P] ss.16, 32-33 |
| **Officer in Charge** | Verifies every warrant before admitting anyone (s.58); keeps the records, stores and valuables (s.30); produces prisoners to court on order (s.67); files the list of prisoners awaiting trial with the High Court before each session (s.118); sees any prisoner who asks, every working day; keeps the Visiting Justices' Book and the visitor's book | [P], daily hearing [S] |
| **Outside oversight** | Visiting Justices (UHRC, judges, magistrates, the district CAO, the IGG, ministers) may enter any prison at any time, test the food, inspect any record, hear complaints, and write in the Visiting Justices' Book; extracts go to the CGP | [P] ss.109-112 |

**What this means for the product:** there are three working levels, not two. Today Pleadli Prisons has a facility and a "Head Office". The region, where the Commander actually supervises 14 or so prisons, does not exist in the app at all.

## 3. A prison's working life (what the facility app is for)

Read as the questions an Officer in Charge and their officers face, in the order a person passes through the prison.

| Moment | What the law asks | Where it is in the app today |
|---|---|---|
| **Someone arrives** | Check the warrant names this person and is properly issued (s.58(2)); refuse a person severely tortured or in bad health (s.58(3)); a clerical error is corrected, not refused (s.58(4)); no juvenile in an adult prison (s.58(7)); search, record property, health screen | Reception procedure built in the node; a stepper exists; reached from "+" |
| **The legal authority** | One of four instruments: commitment, remand warrant, order of detention, warrant of conviction. It must stay true for the whole stay | Instruments with verify, pending, superseded: built, buried in the file |
| **Remand clock** | Constitution Art. 23(6): released on bail after 60 days on remand (180 for offences triable only by the High Court) | Computed; now a red sidebar folder "Past the bail limit" |
| **Court** | Produce on the court's order (s.67), 1,726 a day nationally; before each High Court session, file the list of prisoners awaiting trial (s.118) | Court diary and outcomes built; the s.118 list prints as a report; no "today's court run" |
| **Sentence** | Term, start, remission (one third), release date by calendar, never a day count; four-year reviews for long terms (s.88); parole (s.89) | Clocks built in the node; not shown as a person's "release date" anywhere obvious |
| **Daily life** | One hour in the open; work and farms; rehabilitation and education; religion (s.113); health and hospital referrals; property; visits; letters | Visits, property, health movements, programmes: built as separate pages |
| **Asking and complaining** | The OIC sees any prisoner who asks; application book; Mukutano (prisoners' committee); Visiting Justices | Requests and grievances: built as a table |
| **Order** | Incidents, escapes (search, notify police, re-arrest warrant s.79), discipline | Not built [U]: provisional |
| **Moving to another prison** | Ordered by the CGP (s.73), keep-in-area preference; dossier travels, receipt returns | Two-ended transfer built |
| **Leaving** | Release on entitlement or court order, before noon, never a day late (overholding is unlawful detention); property returned | Discharge gates built; discharge certificate prints |
| **The month** | Monthly statistics: population by convict, remand, debtor; occupancy; productions; releases; escapes; deaths; staff by rank | Monthly return built and attested; sent to Head Office |

**Honest summary:** the node already does most of the custody work. What is missing is not mostly features: it is that **the app does not read like the prison's day**. A person's file does not open with "held on remand since 3 March, next in court 12 April at Kagadi, day 38 of 60". Today does not say "14 to court this morning, 2 releases due, 1 past the bail limit". The Officer in Charge's own duties (s.118 list, Visiting Justices' Book, the daily hearing of prisoners) are not places in the app.

Still unknown and **not to be invented** [U]: the unlock and lock-up procedure (the count), shift handover, the gate register, the Prisons Regulations 2012 full text. These need field input from a prison (backlog R-03).

## 4. What Headquarters does (what the Head Office app is for)

Today's Head Office is, in the user's word, a mock-up: an Estate page, a list of facilities, reports and accounts. That is because it was built to one rule: "HQ aggregates, never records". The rule is right for prisoner records (a prison owns its people's files). But HQ has real work of its own, and none of it is in the app:

| HQ work | Grounded in | In the app |
|---|---|---|
| **See the whole Service today**: population, occupancy, remand share, by region and prison | UPS monthly statistics | Partly: figures per facility, no regions |
| **Act on what cannot wait**: who is past the bail limit and where, overholding, deaths in custody, escapes, silent prisons | Art. 23(6); s.79; UHRC complaints | Counts only, no names, no "where" |
| **Transfers between prisons** (CGP orders) | s.73 | Designed, not built on HQ side |
| **Decisions on people**: parole (s.89), licence (s.87), four-year reviews to the Minister (s.88) | ss.87-89 | Not built |
| **Rules sent down**: standing orders, administrative instructions, prohibited-articles list | ss.7(2), 77(2), 101 | Not built |
| **Returns and statistics**: receive each prison's monthly return, publish the national abstract | UPS practice | Receive and attest built; national abstract not |
| **Oversight**: Visiting Justices' extracts and Judges' and Ministers' observations arrive and are answered | ss.110-111 | Not built |
| **Staff**: establishment and postings, promotion records for the Station, Regional and General Promotion Boards and the Council | ss.10, 14; UPS news | Accounts only |
| **The region**: a Regional Commander's view of their prisons | ss.16, 32-33 | Not built |

The privacy line (what crosses from a prison to HQ) stays a decision for the user, as it was for Police (`docs/police/research/command-levels.md`): to act on "1 person past the bail limit", HQ needs who and where. Section 9 asks.

## 5. What using Pleadli does for the Uganda Prisons Service (the impact)

Said the way it would be said to the Commissioner General:

1. **Nobody is held a day longer than the law allows.** Every release date, remand limit and court date is computed from the warrant, not remembered. The Officer in Charge sees it before it is late, and so does the Regional Commander.
2. **36,000 people on remand are each known.** Past the 60 or 180 days, waiting for papers, not produced at their last court date: each is a name in a folder, not a figure in a report.
3. **The court gets its list, and its people.** The s.118 list prints itself before each session; today's court run (who goes where, under which escort) is one page; the outcome comes back into the file.
4. **The monthly figures count themselves.** The return is produced from the records, attested by the OIC, and reaches Headquarters the same day, even from a prison that is offline most of the time.
5. **Every change is on the record.** Who admitted, who released, who changed a warrant and why: a tamper-evident audit, which is what the Data Protection and Privacy Act 2019 and the UHRC both ask for.
6. **The prison keeps working with no internet.** The records live in the prison, encrypted; nothing ordinary depends on a connection.
7. **It joins the chain.** Police, DPP, Courts and Lawyers are Pleadli apps too: the warrant arrives with the person, the release order comes from the court, the family follows the case with a tracking code on pleadli.com ("Remanded at Kagadi Prison"), without names or evidence.
8. **It costs the Service no new system.** It works beside PMIS, not against it, on the computers prisons already have.

## 6. How Apple would organise it

The rules the other apps already follow (`docs/platform/COMPLETE-DPP-LAWYERS.md`, `docs/police/APPLE-PASS.md`): name places the way the people who work there name them; one primary action per screen; folders with counts; a record opens as one calm page with what matters now on top; no field the officer does not need; nothing empty without saying why and what to do.

### Facility (the prison)

```
Today                          what is owed today, one action: Receive someone
Inbox
PRISONERS                      folders with counts (built 2026-10-08)
  Everyone held · On remand · Past the bail limit · Convicted
  Civil debtors · Papers missing · Released or moved
THE DAY
  Court                        today's court run; the diary; the s.118 list
  Movements                    transfers in and out; hospital
  Visits                       the gate today; booked visits
  Requests                     application book; Mukutano; complaints
THE PRISON
  Registers and papers         every statutory book, printable; Visiting Justices' Book
  Statistics                   the month; the return to Head Office
  Officers                     nominal roll by rank; who is on duty
```

**The person's file**, opened from any folder, as Police's person page now is: photograph, name, prisoner number; one status line ("On remand since 3 Mar 2026 · day 38 of 60 · next in court 12 Apr, Kagadi Chief Magistrate"); one context action (Produce to court, Record outcome, Discharge); segments About | Legal | Life (visits, property, health, programmes) | Timeline | Papers.

**Today** answers, in this order: who cannot wait (past a limit, release due, overheld); who goes to court today; who arrives and leaves; then the figures that are not zero.

### Head Office (the Service)

```
Today                          across the Service: what cannot wait, by prison
Inbox
THE SERVICE
  Regions and prisons          19 regions, each prison with its population and occupancy
  Transfers                    ordered, in transit, received
  Decisions                    parole, licence, four-year reviews
  Oversight                    Visiting Justices' extracts, observations, complaints
HEADQUARTERS
  Directives                   standing orders, instructions, prohibited articles
  Returns and statistics       monthly returns in; the national abstract out
  Staff                        establishment, postings, promotion boards
```

A **Regional Commander** opens the same Head Office app limited to their region (a role, not a new kind of installation): their 14 prisons, their decisions, their committee.

## 7. Countries (jurisdiction packs)

The core (person, custody, warrant, court, transfer, release, records) is the same everywhere. A **pack** holds each country's law: instruments, legal categories, remand limits, sentence and remission rules, registers and returns, ranks, terminology and language. **Germany is removed** (East Africa only, user 2026-10-07). **DR Congo is added**: it was missing.

| Country | Law in force | Who runs prisons | Held | Remand | Occupancy | Pack state |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Uganda** | Prisons Act 2006; Constitution Art. 23(6) (60/180 days) | Uganda Prisons Service, Ministry of Internal Affairs | 79,186 (Nov 2025) | 45.8% | 341.6% | **Built**, figures inline in the engine; move into a pack |
| **Kenya** | Prisons Act, Cap. 90, still listed in force on Kenya Law. A **Correctional Services Bill 2025** is in public consultation (Maraga taskforce). Our Kenya page's claim of a "2024 Act" is **not confirmed** | Kenya Prisons Service, Ministry of Interior | 60,740 (2025 average) | 40.3% | 176.5% | [U] Read Cap. 90 and the Bill |
| **DR Congo** | Ordonnance 344 of 17 Sept 1965 (régime pénitentiaire); Code de procédure pénale (décret 6 Aug 1959) arts 27-29: preventive detention authorised by the tribunal de paix judge; Constitution Art. 18 (garde à vue 48 hours) | Direction des services pénitentiaires, Ministry of Justice; the director (the "keeper") keeps the registers | 44,536 (Dec 2022) | about 70% | 322.8% | [U] French; read the Ordonnance and CPP |
| **Tanzania** | Prisons Act, Cap. 58 (1967); remission rules (GN 26/2003) | Tanzania Prisons Service, Ministry of Home Affairs | 23,940 (Dec 2025) | 28.7% | about 109% | [U] |
| **Rwanda** | Law No. 34/2010 establishing Rwanda Correctional Service, amended by Law 19bis/2017 | Rwanda Correctional Service, Ministry of the Interior | 71,561 (Aug 2025) | 14% | 140.7% | [U] |
| **Burundi**, **South Sudan** | not yet read | | | | | [U] |

Three things change most between countries, so they lead each pack: **(1) the remand clock** (Uganda counts days to mandatory bail; DR Congo's preventive detention is confirmed by a judge and renewed; Kenya and Tanzania differ again); **(2) the warrant and its words** (French for DR Congo, Burundi; "committal" in Kenya); **(3) the monthly return** (each service reports different figures to its ministry).

## 10. Plan (after D1 to D7)

1. **Facility, the day:** Today rebuilt (who cannot wait, court today, arrivals and releases); the person's file as one page with the status line; Court as a place (court run, diary, s.118 list); Registers and papers (statutory books, Visiting Justices' Book). Seen in Chrome and the window.
2. **Head Office, the Service:** Today across the Service; Regions and prisons; Transfers (s.73) end to end; Returns and the national abstract; then Decisions (ss.87-89), Directives (s.101 list, standing orders), Oversight, Staff; the Regional Commander role.
3. **Packs:** Uganda's rules out of the engine into `ug`; then Kenya and DR Congo researched from primary text and written as packs.
4. **Field questions** for a real prison: the count, handover, gate, incidents, the forms they actually use.
